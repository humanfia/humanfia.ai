last two. Every one of PutnamBench's **672** formal statements has a Lean 4 proof that the
PutnamBench team accepted, and the
[official leaderboard](https://trishullab.github.io/PutnamBench/leaderboard.html) lists
Humanfia at 672 ([trishullab/PutnamBench#350](https://github.com/trishullab/PutnamBench/pull/350),
merged 1 September 2026).

## How the top of the board filled up

PutnamBench went from tens of proofs to all of them in about a year. The board's best Lean score
passed 600 in December 2025 and sat at Aleph Prover's 668 from January until the end of August,
when two entries reached 672 a day apart. Ours was the second.

The costs come from each team's own note on the leaderboard, and they are not measured the same
way: one is a total, one is actor-side only. Read them as orders of magnitude, not as a
ranking.<Sidenote>Forall reports $2,364.39 actor-side in total and says reviewer and

Read that way, the benchmark is saturated, and **what separates the leaders now is the price of
a proof, which is where we are not first.** Two of the four got to 672 for a tenth of our cost or
less.

The board also has a separate "no answer" column, where the prover is not given the answer to
problems that ask for one. There, [Midas Prover](https://mi.inc/blog/midas-prover-solves-putnambench)
reports 672 at an average of $6.17 per problem. Our run is in the column with answers, so the
two are not the same test.

## The loop: a worker, a reviewer, and Lean between them

The model that writes a proof never decides whether it is accepted. A worker writes the Lean
file; Lean and Comparator check it; a separate reviewer, which cannot touch the file, asks
the AXLE service for a verdict. Both are `gpt-5.6-sol` at `xhigh`, in separate processes with
separate prompts and separate state.

## What counts as a proof

A problem counts only when the Lean file passes every gate in the
[solver's verification method](https://github.com/humanfia/hoa-qed/tree/main/putnambench#verification-method).
A candidate that fails any of them is kept for inspection, and never counted.

<PostCards numbered>
<PostCard title="The statement is the benchmark's.">

The problem's statement is taken from the pinned `putnam_bench.jsonl` by exact ID, and the
workspace file must match it byte for byte.

</PostCard>
<PostCard title="And it stays the benchmark's.">

A validator confirms the theorem and its docstring are unchanged. Weakened or restated theorems
are rejected.

</PostCard>
<PostCard title="No escapes.">

No `sorry`, `admit`, `axiom` or `native_decide`. The last is out because it trusts the compiler
rather than the kernel.

</PostCard>
<PostCard title="It compiles.">

Against the pinned Lean 4.27.0 and Mathlib.

</PostCard>
<PostCard title="Comparator agrees.">

[Comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator) checks the statement, restricts the
axioms to the standard three, and replays the proof through the Lean kernel. The worker cannot
edit it or its configuration.

</PostCard>
<PostCard title="AXLE agrees.">

A separate reviewer, which cannot edit the file, gets `okay: true` back from the
[AXLE](https://axle.axiommath.ai/) verification API.

</PostCard>
<PostCard title="An audit after the fact.">

Every pass is re-checked afterwards: hashes against the stored AXLE response, the statement
validator again, and a clean rebuild.

</PostCard>
</PostCards>

The worker's shell has no network access, and no existing PutnamBench solution is ever mounted
into either model's workspace.

## Checking it

At the PutnamBench authors' request the full proof set is not public. The
[first 12 proofs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/humanfia-lab/putnambench-solution-preview)
are, along with the whole pipeline, and checking them through AXLE takes Python 3 and a network
connection: the steps are in the
[repository](https://github.com/humanfia/hoa-qed/tree/main/putnambench#start-here-verify-our-proofs-through-the-axle-api).
