All six problems of the 2026 International Mathematical Olympiad, solved by a fully agentic,
YOLO-style run with no human in the turn, and every solution **formally verified by Lean 4**.
There is no rubric here and no benefit of the doubt: the kernel accepts the proof or it does not.

Two workers were run independently, and both closed all six.

## The loop

The formal statements are AxiomMath's, released with
[their own IMO 2026 run](https://github.com/AxiomMath/IMO2026). A worker writes a Lean proof
against each; Lean checks it, and the [AXLE](https://axle.axiommath.ai/) verifier confirms that
the proof proves the statement it was given.

## The times

**API time** is the time actually spent inside model calls, the one measure that does not reward
a slower harness. Here it is against the times AxiomProver reported on the same statements. Compare
against AxiomProver, then hover a problem to read the minutes saved or lost.

On Q1 and Q4, the two easiest problems, we lose. On Q3, the hardest, GPT-5.6 wins by more than
four times. A loop earns its keep exactly where the work is long enough for the loop to matter;
on a problem a strong model closes in twenty minutes, the arrangement around it is overhead.

> A loop earns its keep exactly where the work is long enough for the loop to matter.
